# Suspense & Trailer Structure

The reusable directing playbook for ominous-reveal and trailer work. This is what
makes a creature teaser (e.g. the dragon project) repeatable for ANY creature —
swap the subject, keep the beats. Each beat maps to one FLUX 3 clip; chain them
with the `video-generation` skill's continuation mechanics.

---

## The six suspense principles
Distilled from Jaws / Jurassic Park analyses
(https://thedailyjaws.com/blog/what-jaws-teaches-us-about-tension-and-suspense):

1. **Withhold.** "It is not what you show — it is what you don't show." Shadows,
   sounds, splashes, partial glimpses. Imagination out-scares pixels.
2. **Sound precedes image.** The two-note theme *is* the shark. Wing beats, distant
   roar, stone cracking — the threat exists in audio before it exists visually.
3. **Calm before rupture.** Long quiet beats make the break violent. Pacing = slowly
   rising pressure, not constant action.
4. **Delay the full reveal past the midpoint.** The longer the wait, the sharper the impact.
5. **The camera is the predator.** POV glides, low angles at waterline/ground level.
6. **Escalate in steps, not all at once.** Each beat adds one new piece of information.

## The 8-beat ominous-reveal table
One row = one clip. Shot/movement/lighting/audio columns fill the prompt anatomy.

| # | Beat | Shot / movement | Lighting | Audio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Establishing calm | Aerial extreme-wide drifting over mountain range, slow | Golden hour → fading to blue hour; low fog in valleys | Wind, distant birds, near-silence |
| 2 | Wrongness enters | Static wide; something subtle moves / birds scatter | Light dies, clouds swallow the sun | Sound drops out — silence as signal |
| 3 | First evidence, no creature | Tracking low along scree; claw gouges, crushed trees, shadow passes over frame | Long shadows, low-key | Deep wing-beat whoosh, off-screen |
| 4 | Partial glimpse | ECU on an eye / scale / tail disappearing behind ridge; rack focus from foreground rock to movement | Rim light on scales, silhouette against storm sky | Low rumble building |
| 5 | Human-scale dread (if used) | CU push-in on a witness face; their eyeline leads the cut | Firelight practical, flicker | Breath, heartbeat |
| 6 | The turn | Camera tilts up (Jurassic Park move) or pull-out reveals the shape that was "part of the mountain" | Volumetric light through wing membrane; chiaroscuro | Score swell or full roar |
| 7 | The reveal | Arc/orbit around the full creature, or crane up as wings unfold across the sky | Backlit silhouette breaking into rim-lit detail | Roar + music peak |
| 8 | Sting | Hard cut to black; one final sound (screech, wing beat) and title | — | Sub-bass hit |

User-taste note: restraint with ONE clear glimpse of the subject (e.g. the face held
for a beat, then gone) — not pure implication, not a full lingering reveal. Confirm
the glimpse level before locking the storyboard.

## Trailer templates (Derek Lieu)
From trailer editor Derek Lieu (Matrix trilogy house):
https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2017/9/10/the-matrix-is-a-trailer-editors-dream and
https://www.filmeditingpro.com/trailers-teasers-promos-lengths-formats-tips/
Core rule: energy must VARY — "if a trailer feels high energy all the time, none of
it feels high energy."

### Template 1 — Classic 4-act (2–2.5 min)
1. **Cold open (0:00–0:15):** One exciting/ominous self-contained scene needing zero
   context. Raise a question. (Trinity's rooftop run.) → *Dragon: lone mountain wide,
   wing sound, cut to black.*
2. **Escalation (0:15–1:00):** Introduce the threat/problem. Shots lengthen then
   compress. Dialogue lines that "provide their own context." → *Evidence montage:
   gouges, shadows, fleeing herds.*
3. **Climax (1:00–2:00):** Fast-cutting montage of spectacle, rhythmically edited to
   music hits; visuals literal to any dialogue nouns. → *Glimpses accelerate: eye,
   claw, wing edge, fire light.*
4. **Button / sting (final 5s):** Cut to black → one final perfect line or image →
   title → sub-bass hit. → *Full silhouette across the moon, one roar, title.*

### Template 2 — Teaser / ominous-reveal (30–60s, fits FLUX 3's 5–20s clips chained)
1. **Cold open:** 1 shot, atmospheric, unsettling, no creature.
2. **Rising tension:** 3–5 shots, each closer/more revealing, sound design carrying
   dread; pace accelerates.
3. **Reveal:** 1 shot only — the full creature, held briefly.
4. **Sting:** Black frame + single sound + title card.

This is the default for FLUX 3 work: it maps cleanly onto a handful of chained 5–8s
clips. Use the 8-beat table to populate the rising-tension shots.

## Pacing mechanics
- **Shot-length ramp:** start 4–8s shots, halve each beat until 0.5–1s flashes at the
  climax, then one long held reveal.
- **Silence as a beat:** kill all audio 1–2s before the reveal hit.
- **Withhold ratio:** creature visible <15% of total runtime; everything else is
  evidence and reaction.
- **Audio leads video:** the next shot's sound enters before its picture (J-cut) to
  stitch FLUX clips seamlessly.

## Applying it to any creature
1. Pick Template 2 (teaser) unless the brief asks for a full trailer.
2. Name the creature; research its camera-visible specifics (silhouette, scale,
   texture, signature sound) — grounding.
3. Fill the 8-beat table's shot/lighting/audio columns, swapping dragon-specific
   nouns (wing membrane, claw gouges) for the creature's own evidence.
4. Size each beat to a 5–8s clip; apply the shot-length ramp across the chain.
5. Lock one style prefix and repeat it verbatim across every clip + continuation.
